Companies that suffer repeated hacking incidents can now face a penalty surcharge of <mark>up to 3% of related revenue</mark>
Korea's amended Network Act and its Enforcement Decree took effect on October 1, 2026

The act was promulgated on March 31, and the decree was promulgated on September 30 and took effect the same day

## Penalty surcharge for repeat incidents

![Base rates of the repeat-breach penalty surcharge by severity of violation](/assets/images/tech/network-act-repeat-breach-penalty/en/02-chart.webp)
*Base rates of the repeat-breach penalty surcharge by severity of violation — Source: self-rendered based on the Network Act Enforcement Decree*

The surcharge applies to operators that cause **two or more breach incidents within five years through intent or gross negligence**
The cap is 3% of the revenue related to the incident

The base rate is split into four bands by the severity of the violation
Very serious cases get 2.1–2.7%, and minor ones 0.03–0.9%

Revenue is <mark>calculated excluding revenue unrelated to the incident</mark>
If related revenue is ₩1 trillion, 3% is ₩30 billion

The Ministry of Science and ICT's press release does not say whether incidents before the effective date count toward the five-year tally
With incidents following one after another in September and early October, such as in the financial sector, Hollys, and Malgeunsoft, how the supplementary provisions apply is drawing attention

## Compliance fines and incident investigation

If a company does not follow a government correction order, a **compliance fine** is imposed
The daily base amount is <mark>0.02% of average daily revenue</mark>

Depending on the effort to comply, the base amount can be raised or lowered by up to half
After 90 days past the compliance deadline, it is imposed again every 90 days

A company with average daily revenue of ₩1 billion would be fined ₩200,000 a day

The Ministry of Science and ICT has set up a new **Breach Incident Investigation Review Committee**
Companies with incidents are reviewed through both written materials and on-site inspection

## CISO and information security committee

The chief information security officer, or **CISO**, now has to be an executive
The role goes to a CEO, a board director, or someone who effectively acts as an executive

Some medium-sized companies can designate the head of the information security department as CISO
Operators that appointed a CISO under the old criteria must reappoint one <mark>within six months from October 1</mark>

Covered operators must set up an **information security committee** chaired by the CISO
It meets at least once a year, reports results to the CEO, and puts important items before the board

## Enhanced ISMS certification

| Subject to enhanced certification | Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| Major telecom operators, data center facilities | Revenue of **₩1 trillion** or more |
| Other information and communications service providers | Revenue of ₩3 trillion or more |
| Operators with an incident history | A joint public-private investigation or a repeat-incident surcharge within 3 years |

Enhanced certification has stricter review criteria than the existing **ISMS** certification
On top of documents, it includes on-site and technical reviews

The decree also provides <u>grounds for revoking</u> ISMS certification when breach incidents repeat

## Notifying users and remedying damage

| When users must be notified | Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| Service outage | **2 hours** or more |
| User information | Leaked, lost, altered, misused, or at risk of it |
| Other incidents | Serious risk to information protection |

If there is no contact information, posting on the website for <mark>30 days or more</mark> can stand in for the notification duty

Within **14 days** of reporting an incident, the company must report what it has done to remedy damage
The deadline can be extended by 14 days once

If personal data leaks, the notification duty under the Personal Information Protection Act also applies separately
The rule requiring notice within 72 hours even when a leak is only possible was covered in a September post

The Network Act, under the Ministry of Science and ICT, deals with breach incidents, while the Personal Information Protection Act, under the PIPC, deals with personal data leaks
A single incident can bring reporting duties and surcharges under both laws
