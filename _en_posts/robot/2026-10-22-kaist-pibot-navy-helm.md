An experiment in which KAIST's humanoid **PIBOT** took on the helmsman role on a Navy ship is drawing attention again
After KAIST's Physical AI Korea 2026 event on October 13, reports said it had piloted a warship

The experiment KAIST and the Navy described was <mark>in a land-based simulator, not at sea</mark>

## The experiment

![PIBOT operating the helm on the officer of the deck's orders](/assets/images/robot/kaist-pibot-navy-helm/02-pibot.webp)
*PIBOT operating the helm on the officer of the deck's orders — Source: KAIST, ROK Navy*

The experiment took place on July 23, 2026 at the **Navy Education and Training Command's ship-handling training center**
It was stage 1, a land-based simulation, run inside equipment that mimics the operating environment of a real ship

PIBOT performed the role of the **helmsman**, who holds the course on the bridge
When the officer of the deck gave a voice order, it repeated it back and turned the helm itself

When it reached the target course, <mark>it even reported completion on its own</mark>
An example order was "left 5 degrees rudder," with a target course of 330 degrees

Over about an hour, it was tested in turn under conditions simulating a narrow channel passage, rough weather on the open sea, and night navigation
The research team is analyzing data on command recognition time, helm control precision, and ease of use for operators

## A design that uses the crew's existing controls

![Concept shot of a robot seated in a human cockpit operating the controls as is](/assets/images/robot/kaist-pibot-navy-helm/03-agy.webp)
*Concept shot of a robot seated in a human cockpit operating the controls as is — Source: concept shot · self-generated with agy*

What sets PIBOT apart is that <mark>it does not modify the ship's existing controls</mark>
Without retrofitting existing equipment, the robot takes over a human crew member's physical duties

PIBOT uses an **LLM (large language model)** to understand and carry out ship-handling procedures and voice orders
It operates the cockpit, helm, and switches people use directly with its hands

PIBOT was originally known as an airplane-piloting robot
In July 2023, it was announced to have done startup, taxiing, takeoff, landing, and cruising in a flight simulator

Professor Shim Hyun-chul described it as a case of university technology contributing to field missions as military manpower shrinks
He said they would expand the range of missions the robot performs, starting with ship handling

## The 7,000-ton warship report versus the official announcement

![PIBOT research timeline](/assets/images/robot/kaist-pibot-navy-helm/en/04-chart.webp)
*PIBOT research timeline — Source: self-rendered based on KAIST, ROK Navy, and press reports*

In its October 13 event report, Money Today said PIBOT piloted a 7,000-ton-class Navy ship in July
KAIST's official post and the Navy's announcement **did not disclose any tonnage**

According to the official announcements, the July experiment was in a land-based simulator
Steering trials on an actual ship begin with <mark>stage 2, trials on a moored ship</mark>

| Stage 2 | Moored ship | Planned |

## Project size and schedule

PIBOT is a project under the Agency for Defense Development's **Future Challenge Defense Technology R&D program**
The Defense Acquisition Program Administration has funded <mark>₩5.7 billion</mark>, and KAIST has been researching it since 2022

Professor Kim Min-jun of the School of Electrical Engineering is also part of the research
KAIST and the Navy have not announced specific dates for stage 2 and beyond

At the October 13 event, Professor Park Hae-won's team's humanoid doing a moonwalk and Professor Kong Kyoung-chul's team's wearable robot were also shown
